THE EXPERIMENT

What is The Autonomous Account?

The Autonomous Account is a real-money experiment in automated trading.

We built a software system, gave it a small amount of real capital and a fixed set of risk rules, and then allowed it to find and execute trades without us choosing each trade manually.

The purpose is not to prove that AI can magically beat the market. It is to find out what actually happens when a trading system is given enough independence to make its own decisions, while still operating inside strict safety limits.

Every completed production trade is logged. Wins and losses are both included. The results shown on this site come from real trades using real money.

What are we trying to learn?

The interesting part is not whether the first few trades make money. Over time, we want enough data to understand when the system works, when it fails and why.

  • Does it perform better in strong trends than sideways markets?
  • Does it favour certain assets?
  • Are there times of day when its signals are stronger?
  • How much performance disappears through fees and slippage?
  • How severe are losing streaks?
  • Are some strategies genuinely complementary to others?

Those questions need evidence, not guesswork.

Where this is going

This is the first strategy. The longer-term plan is to operate several independently tested trading systems, each using a different approach to the market. Their trades, returns and failures can then be compared over meaningful samples.

Rather than constantly changing a strategy whenever it loses, each version will be allowed to collect enough data to judge it properly.

Successful ideas can then be refined. Weak ideas can be rejected. Different strategies may eventually be combined if the evidence suggests they perform well under different market conditions.

In other words, this is less a search for a magical trading bot and more an attempt to build a small autonomous trading laboratory.

The rules matter

The system does not have unlimited freedom. Its trading activity operates inside predetermined risk controls, including limits on risk per trade, concurrent positions and total exposure.

Stops and protective orders form part of the execution process. These controls exist independently of whether the trading strategy is currently winning or losing.

Why publish the results?

Because an experiment is more interesting when failure cannot quietly disappear.

The public record updates from confirmed completed production trades. Losing trades remain part of the record.

No trading signals or current positions are published here, and this project is not offering investment advice or a trading service.

It is simply an experiment.

  1. Build the system.
  2. Give it real money.
  3. Record what happens.
  4. Learn from the evidence.
