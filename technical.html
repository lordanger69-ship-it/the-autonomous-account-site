TECHNICAL METHODOLOGY

Introduction

The Autonomous Account is an experimental framework for evaluating autonomous systematic trading strategies using small amounts of real capital.

The objective is to give strategies meaningful samples under stable rules, while recording execution, risk, market conditions and outcomes for later analysis. A handful of profitable trades is too small a sample to optimise around.

Risk architecture

Normal production risk per trade
0.75% of verified account equity
Absolute software ceiling
3%, a failsafe above normal position sizing
Maximum concurrent positions
3
Maximum gross portfolio leverage
3×

No averaging down. No widening stops. If the calculated position is too small to satisfy an exchange minimum, the system skips the trade.

Unknown or manually created exposure blocks new autonomous entries until the system can reconcile the account state.

Safety and execution

Strategy decisions and hard risk enforcement are separate. The trading system cannot control funding, withdrawals or transfers.

The system establishes and verifies exchange-side stop-loss and take-profit protection and keeps durable records of intended actions, exchange fills and reconciliation evidence.

If account state becomes uncertain, the system blocks new entries. A mark-to-market high-water-mark circuit breaker blocks new entries after a 30% equity drawdown while preserving management of existing positions.

There is deliberately no daily drawdown stop. The hypothesis is that an automated strategy does not suffer behavioural problems such as revenge trading or FOMO. Long-run evidence may support or reject that decision.

Experimental method

V1 should remain substantially frozen while an adequate live sample accumulates. Changing parameters after every losing trade would introduce hindsight and overfitting.

Analysis should eventually include:

  • Expectancy
  • Average R
  • Win rate
  • Payoff ratio
  • Drawdown
  • Volatility
  • Trading fees
  • Slippage
  • Market-specific results
  • Long versus short performance
  • Time-of-day effects
  • Performance under different market regimes

Operational failures are also data. Reconciliation failures, exchange behaviour, rejected orders and protection issues matter because a profitable signal is of limited value if the surrounding execution system is unreliable.

Multi-strategy roadmap

The longer-term architecture is intended to support several isolated strategies simultaneously. Likely research systems include:

V1
Breakout / trend
V2

V3
Mean reversion
V4
Shorter-term momentum

Each strategy should have versioned rules and an independent performance history. We want to determine whether different systems perform under different market conditions.

A future higher-level allocation system may only be considered after individual strategies have accumulated sufficient evidence.

Public reporting

The public website is separate from live trading infrastructure. It receives only a sanitised static dataset generated from confirmed production records.

The site has no signing capability, trading credentials, ability to submit orders, or access to current positions or live trading signals.

Public reporting may include completed trades, realised performance, periodic account-balance snapshots and historical performance charts.

Current positions, open orders, stop levels, take-profit levels, signals, wallet information and operational controls remain private.

MEASURE FIRST. MODIFY SECOND.

A strategy that loses several trades has not necessarily failed. A strategy that wins several trades has not necessarily succeeded.

The value of The Autonomous Account comes from accumulating enough controlled evidence to distinguish genuine behaviour from ordinary market variance.
